# The Woman Who Fooled Doctors With Rabbits (Mary Toft, 1726)

A 60 s vertical short in the detailed style. Narration: Kokoro `am_fenrir`, speed 1.15. Staged tastefully: Mary stays in bed, cartoon bunnies hop about, and the joke is on the gullible doctors. Nothing graphic is shown.

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| In 1726 Mary Toft of Godalming, Surrey, claimed to give birth to rabbits and fooled several doctors | Wikipedia, Public Domain Review, Linda Hall Library |
| Local surgeon John Howard took up her case and spread the news | Wikipedia, Univ. of Glasgow |
| King George I sent his surgeon-anatomist Nathaniel St André (with Samuel Molyneux) | Wikipedia, Univ. of Glasgow |
| On arrival St André was told she was in labour with her 15th rabbit, and he believed it | Univ. of Glasgow, Wikipedia |
| She was moved to London (Lacy's Bagnio, Leicester Fields), where crowds were curious | Public Domain Review, Wikipedia |
| A porter reported that she had tried to bribe him to bring rabbits into her room | Public Domain Review, Wikipedia |
| Sir Richard Manningham threatened a painful procedure, and she confessed (early December 1726) | Wikipedia, Linda Hall Library |
| She was charged as a "Notorious and Vile Cheat", jailed in Bridewell, later released, and went home to Godalming | Public Domain Review, Wikipedia |
| The doctors were ridiculed, including in Hogarth's 1726 print *Cunicularii* | Univ. of Glasgow, UCL Museums, Wellcome Collection |

Claims that could not be confirmed (for example, Londoners giving up rabbit) are left out. The print in the shop window is our own redrawing, not Hogarth's engraving.

Code: `toon/src/toft` (composition `ToftShort`) plus the shared `toon/src/look`. Audio: `audio/make_audio.py`. Narration: `narration/`.
