# The Day a Giant Wave of Molasses Destroyed a City Neighborhood (Boston, 1919)

A 60 s vertical short in the detailed style. Narration: Kokoro `am_fenrir`, speed 1.15.

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| January 15, 1919, just after lunch (around 12:40 pm), Boston's North End | Britannica, Live Science / APS |
| 50-ft steel tank; about 2.3 million gallons | Boston.gov, NBC, Britannica |
| Wave up to about 25 ft (estimates 15–40 ft), about 35 mph | Wikipedia, Britannica, History.com (Sharp 2014 model) |
| Smashed buildings, damaged the elevated railway | Wikipedia, CBS / Boston Globe photo captions |
| 21 dead, about 150 injured | Britannica, NPR, NBC |
| Cold made the molasses far thicker, trapping people and slowing rescue | APS / ScienceDaily (Sharp, Kennedy & Rubinstein, 2016) |
| Tank leaked; the company painted it brown to hide the leaks | APS News, Live Science |
| 1925 court-appointed auditor: the owners were negligent | Boston.gov |
| Led to stricter construction rules across the country | Britannica, History.com |
| Residents said the area smelled of molasses on hot days for decades | Wikipedia |
| Today a park (ballfield) with a plaque | Boston.gov / CBS |
